Why Is Greenland Icy and Iceland Green?

Watch on YouTube · Subscribe to Map Behind History

Why is Greenland icy and Iceland green? The short answer

Greenland is about 80% ice because the cold East Greenland Current flows down its coast from the Arctic, while Iceland is warmed by the North Atlantic and Irminger currents. The swapped-sounding names come from two Vikings a century apart: Flóki named Iceland after a bad winter, and Erik the Red named Greenland to attract settlers.

The story

Greenland is about 80% ice and Iceland is mostly green, so why do their names sound swapped? The names came from two Vikings a century apart: one bad winter in Iceland and one sales pitch in Greenland.

The real reason one island is frozen is written in the ocean: the warm North Atlantic and Irminger currents on one side, the Arctic East Greenland Current on the other. We also follow the Norse settlers who farmed southern Greenland for about 400 years, and the ice cores that show Greenland has been green before.
