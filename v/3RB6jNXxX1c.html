Why Does Brazil Speak Portuguese? The 1494 Line

Why does Brazil speak Portuguese? The short answer

Brazil speaks Portuguese because of the Treaty of Tordesillas (7 June 1494), which split newly found lands along a line 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands: west for Castile (Spain), east for Portugal. The eastern bulge of South America fell on Portugal's side, and Pedro Álvares Cabral claimed Brazil for Portugal in 1500.

The story

On 7 June 1494, at Tordesillas in Spain, Castile and Portugal agreed a line from pole to pole, 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands: new lands to the west for Castile, to the east for Portugal. It moved the line set by Pope Alexander VI's 1493 bull, 100 leagues out. Later maps placed the line anywhere from about 42° to 50° W.

In 1500 Pedro Álvares Cabral reached Brazil, east of the line. England, the Netherlands and France never recognised the division. In 1529 the Treaty of Zaragoza drew a second line on the other side of the globe.
