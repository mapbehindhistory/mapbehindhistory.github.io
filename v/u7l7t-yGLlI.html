How Istanbul Ended Up on Two Continents

Watch on YouTube · Subscribe to Map Behind History

Why is Istanbul on two continents? The short answer

Istanbul is on two continents because it grew on both shores of the Bosphorus, the narrow strait that divides Europe from Asia. Greek settlers founded Chalcedon on the Asian shore first, then Byzantium on the better European site opposite; the city later spread across the strait, and today about two in three residents live on the European side.

The story

Istanbul spans Europe and Asia across the Bosphorus. Greek settlers from Megara first founded Chalcedon on the Asian shore; according to Strabo, an oracle later told the founders of Byzantium to build “opposite the blind”, which they took to mean Chalcedon, and they settled the far better European site.

The strait narrows to about 700 m and is the sea road between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean. Constantine made the city capital of the Roman Empire in 330; Mehmed II took it in 1453, and it remained an imperial capital until 1922. The first bridge across the Bosphorus opened in 1973.
