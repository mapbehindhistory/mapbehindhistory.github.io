Why Chile Is So Long and Thin

Watch on YouTube · Subscribe to Map Behind History

Why is Chile so long and thin? The short answer

Chile is thin because the Andes mountains run down its whole eastern side, leaving a narrow strip between the peaks and the Pacific. It is long because nearly four centuries of conquest, war and treaties added land to the north and south: the occupation of Araucanía, the Strait of Magellan, the War of the Pacific and the 1902 Andes arbitration with Argentina.

The story

Chile runs 4,270 km from north to south but is only 177 km wide on average. The two halves of that shape have two different explanations: the Andes made Chile thin, and nearly four centuries of conquest, war and treaties made it long.
